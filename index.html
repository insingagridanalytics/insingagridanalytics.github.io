Energy losses · solved by LossFinder

Losses show up in the totals, but not on the map.

Between the transformer and the customer's meter, utilities struggle to measure how much energy goes missing, or to separate technical losses (energy lost as heat in lines and equipment) from non-technical losses (meter faults, billing errors, illegal connections). The result is lost revenue every month, and inspection teams sent to the wrong places.

How we help: Insinga LossFinder checks your network data, measures losses at every transformer from the data you already hold, and gives your crews a ranked list of meters to inspect.

How it works
  1. Data foundation. Cleans, connects and catalogues your network, customer, billing and meter data into one database, kept up to date by automated pipelines.
  2. Data health check. Finds customers linked to the wrong transformer, missing links and duplicate meters, which make loss figures wrong. Engineers approve every correction.
  3. Virtual transformer meters. An energy balance for every transformer, built from GIS, token sales and feeder or transformer readings.
  4. Inspection and revenue recovery. A ranked list of meters to inspect, with the reasons. Engineers approve each visit and track what is recovered.

Stories from the line

We build technology for the grid. We started on it.

Behind every network map are the linemen, engineers and operators who build and repair the grid, often in the rain, at night and at height. We never forget them, because that is where we come from. This is where we share their stories, starting with our own.

Story 01

From the top of the pole to the database

For six years, our co-founder and CEO, Nshuti Elisé, worked as a lineman and electrical engineer on medium- and low-voltage networks at Rwanda Energy Group. He climbed poles to repair faults, installed and repaired customer meters, and walked the lines with a phone to collect the field GIS data that utilities rely on today.

On the ground, he met what the records often miss. A new customer is connected, or a faulty meter replaced, in a hurry or carelessly, and the change never reaches the GIS, or reaches it wrong. Some meters are tampered with so they record less than is used, others are bypassed, and illegal connections hang off the lines. A meter hit by lightning can stop counting while it keeps supplying power, and nobody notices for months. Rain and storms damage other meters and connections, and poor-quality materials end up on the network without the utility knowing. Often nobody can say exactly how many households a transformer supplies. Each problem is small. Added up across a network, they become the energy losses no one can locate.

He then trained in data science at Carnegie Mellon University Africa, where his research mapped and computed energy losses on distribution networks. Every pole, meter and token sale became a record in a spatial database, linked to the transformer that feeds it, and AI could find the patterns no single field visit shows.

That is the idea behind LossFinder: bring the network on the ground and the network in the database back into agreement, then show crews exactly where to act.

FAQ

Common questions

Do we need new meters or sensors?

No. Insinga starts from data you already hold: network GIS, billing, prepaid token sales, meter readings and feeder or transformer readings.

Will our data be used to train models for other utilities?

No. Your data is never used to train shared models. Each utility's results are built from its own data, for its own network.

Who can see our data?

Only the people you approve. Access is set by role, and every action and data release is logged.

Where does Insinga run?

On your own servers, in a local or national cloud, or hosted by us, depending on your data protection and data residency rules.

Does Insinga make decisions on its own?

No. It recommends corrections, plans and inspections. Your engineers and managers review and approve every action.

Can we start small?

Yes. Most utilities start with a Network Data Health Check and LossFinder on one feeder, then extend to more feeders. Modules such as electrification planning and data sharing can be added later on the same data.

What do you need from us to start a pilot?

The network map for the pilot area, a year of prepaid sales or billing data, and feeder or transformer readings if you have them. We agree data handling rules with you before anything is shared.
