The problems we solve

Does this sound like your organisation?

Energy losses

You know energy is being lost, but not where.

Between the transformer and the customer's meter, utilities struggle to measure how much energy goes missing, or to separate technical losses (energy lost as heat in lines and equipment) from non-technical losses (meter faults, billing errors, illegal connections). The result is lost revenue every month, and inspection teams sent to the wrong places.

How we help: we combine network, transformer, meter and billing data with field knowledge of how distribution networks really behave, to measure losses for each transformer area and show where to act first.

You have connection targets, but no reliable plan to reach them.

Ministries and electrification programmes need to know who is still unserved, how far they are from the grid, and whether grid extension, a mini-grid or solar home systems is the cheapest way to reach them.

Data sharing

Researchers and partners need your data, but you can't share it safely.

Utilities rarely have a safe, structured way to share data, so requests stall or raw files are sent without control. Researchers, in turn, have no way to test their methods on real utility data.

How we help: our working prototype lets the utility decide what to share, gives partners approved samples, and runs their algorithms on the full data inside the utility. Only results leave, and everything is logged.

What we do

Most utilities already hold years of GIS, metering and billing records, often in separate systems that don't link to each other. We connect that data, then use it to expand access and cut losses.

Data foundation and sharing

Network data, spatial data engineering and governed sharing

We review MV and LV GIS layers for missing records, duplicates and broken connections, correct the network topology, and trace every customer to its transformer and feeder. We then build the spatial database and automated pipelines that keep GIS files, field surveys and system exports in one connected, up-to-date place.

Once your data is clean, we help you share it safely. Researchers, funders, regulators and vendors regularly ask utilities for data. With our platform, the utility decides what is released and partners receive approved samples only.

How it works
  1. Collect data from public and private energy sector players and integrate it into one GIS.
  2. Map where people live and estimate the electricity needs of households, schools, health centres and businesses.
  3. Map the existing network and where it has capacity for new connections.
  4. Produce a least-cost electrification map with grid extension routes, off-grid zones and a prioritised investment list.
Energy losses

Energy loss reduction

We compare energy sent out with energy billed for each feeder and transformer area, then separate technical losses (energy lost as heat in lines and transformers) from non-technical losses (metering faults, billing errors and illegal connections). We also check whether load is spread evenly across the three phases of each transformer, since uneven phases overheat equipment and add to losses.

What you get
  • Energy balance by feeder and transformer area
  • Loss heat maps with technical and non-technical losses shown separately
  • Inspection priority lists for field teams
  • Phase imbalance by transformer and lists of customers to reconnect
